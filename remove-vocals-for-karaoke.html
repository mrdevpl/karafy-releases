How to remove vocals from a song for karaoke

A karaoke backing track is the song without the lead singer. Here is how vocal removal works today, what result to expect, and how to get a ready karaoke track in one go.

The old trick, and why it rarely works

Older audio programs remove vocals by cancelling out whatever sits in the middle of the stereo mix. The voice is usually there — but so are the bass, the kick drum and often the snare. The result is thin, hollow and still has echoes of the singer.

How AI separation works

Modern tools use a neural network trained on thousands of songs to recognise what a voice sounds like. It splits the recording into two tracks — vocals and everything else — and keeps the drums and bass intact.

What result to expect

  • Studio recordings with a clear lead vocal separate best.
  • Backing vocals, choirs and heavy reverb may stay faintly audible.
  • Live recordings and old, noisy masters are harder: the voice bleeds into the other instruments.

For singing along that faint trace usually doesn't matter — your own voice covers it.
