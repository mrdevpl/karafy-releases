Karaoke software for PC: karaoke at home on Windows

Your computer is already most of a karaoke machine. Here is what kinds of karaoke software exist, what else you need, and how to sing the songs you actually like.

Two kinds of karaoke software

  • Players play karaoke files you already have, or stream songs from a catalog. You sing what the catalog offers.
  • Makers create karaoke files: a backing track without vocals and lyrics that light up in time. You sing any song you have as a file.

Karafy is a maker. It turns your own song files into karaoke and exports them in formats that common players open — or as an MP4 video that plays anywhere.

What you need for karaoke at home

  • A Windows PC or laptop — and, if you can, a TV or projector connected to it, so everyone sees the lyrics.
  • Speakers loud enough for the room.
  • A microphone — a wired or wireless karaoke microphone connected to your speakers or a small mixer is the simplest setup.
  • Songs — karaoke files from a player's catalog, or your own songs turned into karaoke.

Making karaoke from your own songs

  1. Open a song file you own (MP3, WAV, FLAC, M4A, OGG, MP4 or MKV) in Karafy and click Generate.
  2. Karafy removes the vocals, recognises the lyrics and times every word — on your own computer.
  3. Check the lyrics in the built-in editor and fix anything that is off.
  4. Export: MP4 to play in any video player or on a TV, KFN for KaraFun-compatible players, MP3+G for karaoke hardware, or LRC for synced-lyrics apps.

A real example

We made karaoke from “Good to Go” by Josh Woodward (3:09), a song we licensed for our demo, processing locally on a laptop with an AMD Ryzen 5 PRO 215 processor.

  • Processing took 2 min 43 s, less than the song itself.
  • Karafy matched the lyrics against the published version from LRCLIB.
  • In the editor we found one thing to fix: the word “go” had ended up on a line of its own at the end of a phrase.

System requirements

  • Windows 10 or 11
  • A processor with AVX (most Intel CPUs from 2011 and AMD from 2012 onwards)
  • 4 GB of RAM

No graphics card needed and no account. Once a song is made, it plays offline.
